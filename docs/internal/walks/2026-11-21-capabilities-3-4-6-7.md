# Walk of capabilities 3, 4, 6 and 7 — 2026-11-21

Internal record. A verifier walked four first-release capabilities end to end on PrestaShop 8.2.8
(PHP 8.0.30) and 9.2.0: the merchant's part through the admin screens in a browser, the shopper's
part through the storefront in a browser, the background part through the tick URL, and the Smaily
side on the mock behind the egress guard. Nothing in the module was changed.

| Capability | Verdict | Checks passed, 8.2.8 | Checks passed, 9.2.0 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3. Store mode by language (four modes) | **HOLDS** | 15 of 15 | 15 of 15 |
| 4. Store mode by shop | **HOLDS** (defect 1 found beside the named scenarios) | 20 of 20 | 20 of 20 |
| 6. Contact sync both ways, first import, Log with retry | **HOLDS** | 131 of 131 | 131 of 131 |
| 7. Store automations | **HOLDS** | 35 of 35 | 34 of 34 |

The Initial setup that all four rest on: 10 of 10 on each version. Gauge after this walk: 7 / 14.

## 1. How the walk ran

- **Shops.** Two stacks of this worktree, `smaily-presta-walk82` (flashlight 8.2.8-8.0-nginx, port
  8382, theme classic) and `smaily-presta-walk92` (flashlight 9.2.0-nginx, port 8392, theme
  hummingbird), from `build/walk/docker-compose.yaml`: the dev stack without the `src/` mount, the
  module copied from the read-only mount, so that the module folder could hold a genuine 2.2.0
  first. Both removed with `down -v` at the end.
- **Code.** The module of `v3` at `2cbb6fa` for the setup, the reconcile, the first import and the
  consent matrix; at `ffcc87b` (files copied over, same version, no schema change) for the Log, the
  automations, the language modes, the shops and the upgrade. `cad467a` (discount codes, off by
  default) landed afterwards and was not walked.
- **Staging by script, not part of what is proven** (`build/walk/stage.php`): Estonian as a second
  language (no language pack: the Estonian pages show English words), no outgoing mail, the two demo
  customers moved to `example.test`, and for capability 4 a second shop "Second shop" at
  `127.0.0.1:<port>` with the catalog of shop 1 (PrestaShop's own `copyShopData()`, plus the payment
  modules' carrier rows). Shop 1 answers at `localhost:<port>`.
- **Browser.** Headless Chromium through playwright-core 1.60: one context for the employee, a
  fresh context for every shopper. The scripts are in the session's scratch folder
  (`…/scratchpad/w3467/`), not in the repository; every check was appended to a result file per
  version, and this document is written from those files.
- **Smaily.** The mock of the tests behind `tests/egress-guard`, installed and checked before the
  module's first request in every shop. Its state was extended for the walk: 18 workflows, each
  listed to one of the accounts `walkmain`, `walken`, `walket`, `walkshop2` (and two of
  `example-store` for the upgrade), an unsubscriber list, an action log, the mock's own record of
  single contacts, and its two failure switches. The mock accepts one invented API user for every
  subdomain and records the host of each request, which is how the walk tells the accounts apart.
- **Shoppers.** 105 to 126 invented people per shop, all on `example.test`.
- **Nothing left the machine.** The proxy's log at the end of each shop holds only
  `TUNNEL <account>.sendsmaily.net -> mock` lines and `REFUSED CONNECT` lines. Main walk, 8.2.8:
  388 tunnels (walkmain 279, walken 49, walket 12, walkshop2 47, the guard's probe 1), 6 refusals
  (api.github.com, api.prestashop-project.org, the guard's probe). 9.2.0: 300 tunnels, 1150
  refusals, nearly all of modules that the 9.2.0 image ships (a.klaviyo.com, sentry.io,
  mbo-api.prestashop.com). Upgrade shops: 5 tunnels to `example-store` each. `check.sh` answered
  `guard active` at the end of every shop. The browser contexts refused every request to another
  origin than the shop.
- **Crashes.** No 502, no PHP-FPM crash line and no `core` file on either version (more than
  25 000 requests on 8.2.8, 16 000 on 9.2.0). On 8.2.8 eight background requests of PrestaShop's own
  dashboard (`refreshDashboard`, `common/notifications`, `AdminAddresses`) answered 500 in three
  bursts, each within seconds of the walk removing `var/cache` while a freshly loaded dashboard
  still had requests in flight (the three starts of the shop staging). No module route and no
  storefront request is among them, and it did not happen on 9.2.0, where the walk waited for the
  dashboard to go quiet first. The error pages were not read.

No shortcut replaces the thing proven: every setting was saved on a screen, every shopper act was
done in the storefront, every delivery went through the tick or the request's own flush.

Orders were placed with the first payment method the checkout offers (an offline one, so the order
waits for its payment). The first-order automation starts when the order is placed, whatever its
state (ARCHITECTURE 7); "first paid order" in the task was walked as "first placed order".

## 7. The three points to establish

**(a) A newsletter-block subscriber without an account gets the welcome only with "Include guests"
on.** Confirmed on both versions in six configurations (section 3.1, row E3): welcome in
"Subscribers only" and "All customers" with guests on, none with guests off, none in "Checkout
opt-in only". It is what the documents and the code say: ARCHITECTURE 6.2 puts newsletter-block
subscribers under "Include guests" for the contact sync, section 7 starts the welcome only "where
… the shop's consent mode sends the contact", and `Automation\Welcome\WelcomeTrigger` asks
`ContactAudience::syncs()` with the event's source. An upgraded store has guests on, and its block
signup was welcomed (U5). The siblings: Magento syncs and welcomes a newsletter-form subscription
in the consent and legitimate-interest modes whatever its "Include guest order emails" says; that
setting gates only a guest's order address (`Observer/OrderPlaced.php`), and
`Observer/SubscriberSaveAfter.php` has no guest gate. WooCommerce has no signup form without an
account (its welcome sources are the checkout, the block checkout, the registration and My
account); its welcome follows the contact sync's audience, so a guest's checkout tick is welcomed
only where the order path sends the guest. So a person who explicitly subscribed in the block gets
no contact sync and no welcome on a fresh PrestaShop install (guests off by default), where
Magento would send both. The owner's call.

**(b) The welcome on subscribe → unsubscribe → subscribe.** Two welcome triggers on both versions
(W2); the contact writes carried the statuses 0, 1, 0. ARCHITECTURE 7 and Story 4.2 "As built"
describe it; Story 4.2's acceptance 1 says "once". The siblings: Magento starts the welcome on
every storefront subscription, "a resubscription included" (`Observer/SubscriberSaveAfter.php`;
its audit finding R6 asked for once, and PRO-3580 narrowed the welcome to the storefront only).
WooCommerce starts it on each storefront opt-in that changes the consent record, with a dedupe
inside one request only (`HookHandler`). Shopify sends it once per contact, with a record per shop
and address (`WelcomeSent`, DECISIONS S-66). PrestaShop as built matches Magento and WooCommerce.

**(c) The 2.x admin tab after an upgrade.** After a genuine console upgrade from 2.2.0 on both
versions, the 2.2.0 row `SmailyForPrestaShopConfiguration` is still in `ps_tab`, as it was under
2.2.0: inactive, without a parent and without a route. It is not in the back-office menu, which
holds one link, "Smaily Connect", to the Dashboard. The upgrade added the five 3.0.0 rows
(`AdminSmailyConnect` under `AdminParentCustomer`, and Dashboard, Setup, Settings, Log under it).
The row's language and authorization rows were not looked at.

## 8. Defects

1. **A save meant for one shop can replace the setup that all shops share** (both versions).
   The Settings endpoints read the selected shop from the back office's cookie, not from the page
   that posts. When the cookie stands at "All shops", a post with `own: true` is saved to the
   shared scope and answers "Saved." (`Admin\Api\AbstractJsonAction`, the selected shop;
   `Admin\Settings\SettingsScope::scope()`, which falls back to the shared scope).
   *Smallest reproduction:* one store, two shops. Tab A: Settings > Connection with shop 2
   selected. Tab B: select "All shops" on any back-office page. Tab A, not reloaded: tick "Use its
   own Smaily account on this shop", type another account, Save Connection. The account row of
   shop 0 now holds the typed account, shop 2 has no row, and every shop's contacts and triggers go
   to that account. It also happened without a second tab three times in four, when the walk
   selected a shop right after the login: a late answer of PrestaShop's dashboard put the cookie
   back to "All shops" while the page was drawn for shop 2. Shown for the Connection tab; the
   Contacts and Automations tabs take their scope the same way and were not tried.
2. **After "a → single" the automations go to another account with the first account's workflow
   ids** (both versions). Saving mode a writes the fallback language's account as the account of
   no language, without a question (the first account's credentials are gone). Back in single
   mode the cards still hold the workflows picked from the first account's list, the tab marks
   them "(not in your Smaily list — kept)", and they are still started: the walk's signups, orders
   and carts were posted to `walken` with the ids 11, 12 and 13 of `walkmain`.
   *Smallest reproduction:* single mode with account X and a welcome workflow of X; Settings >
   Connection → "Per-language Smaily accounts" with accounts Y and Z, fallback Y, save; → "Single
   language", confirm, save; register with the newsletter box; the mock receives the welcome at Y
   with X's workflow id.
3. **A shopper whose customer was deleted still gets the abandoned-cart reminder** at the address
   typed at the checkout, for up to 24 hours (both versions; section 4, last rows).
   *Smallest reproduction:* abandoned cart on; a visitor types an address at the checkout with the
   box ticked and continues as a guest; delete the customer; let the cart pass the delay; tick.
4. **The Log's Details word a held-back stale subscription wrongly** (seen on 8.2.8): "A later
   message of this kind already reached this contact; sending again would deliver it twice." The
   later row was the contact's unsubscribe; sending again would subscribe the contact again.
   *Reproduction:* section 3.4, Details of the failed row whose later unsubscribe was delivered.
5. **Reason codes that still show as codes in the Log:** `too_old`, `not_in_audience` (STATUS.md
   already lists this work).
